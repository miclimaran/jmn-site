A Certificate of Origin (CoO) states where goods were made. For auto parts leaving Indonesia it comes in two varieties, and which one your shipment needs depends entirely on the destination: a **preferential** form if the destination has a trade agreement with Indonesia, and an **ordinary** form if it does not. Asking for the wrong one is the single most common reason an otherwise complete document set gets rejected at the destination port.

In Indonesia the certificate is called **Surat Keterangan Asal (SKA)** and is issued electronically through the Ministry of Trade's e-SKA system. It is not issued by the exporter. It is issued by an authorised body — an *Instansi Penerbit SKA* (IPSKA) — and the Directorate General of Foreign Trade coordinates with [95 such issuing agencies](https://ditjendaglu.kemendag.go.id/berita/rapat-koordinasi-ska-dengan-95-ipska) nationwide.

## When the form actually saves your buyer money

A preferential CoO lets the importer claim a reduced or zero tariff under a trade agreement. That only works if three things are true at once:

1. The destination country is party to an agreement with Indonesia
2. The goods meet that agreement's rules of origin
3. The correct form accompanies the shipment

Miss any one of them and the importer pays the standard duty rate. The saving is real — on a container of body panels the tariff difference can exceed the freight cost — but it is not automatic, and it is the importer who loses if the paperwork is wrong.

## Which form for which destination

| Form | Agreement | Covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form D | ASEAN Trade in Goods Agreement (ATIGA) | Intra-ASEAN: Malaysia, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Singapore and the rest |
| Form E | ASEAN–China Free Trade Area (ACFTA) | China |
| Form AK | ASEAN–Korea FTA | South Korea |
| Form AI | ASEAN–India FTA (AIFTA) | India |
| Form IJEPA | Indonesia–Japan Economic Partnership | Japan |
| Ordinary CoO | None — non-preferential | Everywhere else |

That last row is the one that matters most for our own shipments. **The Middle East and most of Africa have no preferential agreement with Indonesia**, so parts going to Jeddah, Dubai, Lagos or Dar es Salaam normally travel on an ordinary Certificate of Origin. It still has to be correct — customs use it to confirm the goods are Indonesian and to apply the right duty rate — but there is no preferential tariff to claim, and a buyer who insists on a Form D for a UAE shipment has been given bad advice somewhere upstream.

One practical note on Form E: importers have reported that customs officers in several ASEAN countries ask for the original paper copy rather than a scan, so plan for the physical document to travel or arrive ahead of the goods.

## What the issuing body needs from the exporter

The IPSKA checks the claim before signing, so the application has to be backed by the commercial documents:

- Commercial invoice and packing list
- Bill of lading or airway bill
- HS code classification for each line item
- Export declaration (PEB)
- Evidence supporting the origin claim where a preferential form is requested

For OEM auto parts the HS classification is where most of the friction sits. Parts that look interchangeable to a buyer can fall under different headings — a lamp assembly, a lamp lens and a bulb are not the same classification — and an inconsistency between the invoice, the HS code and the CoO is exactly what a customs officer looks for.

## Who should request it

The exporter applies, not the buyer. If you are importing from Indonesia, the practical step is to tell your supplier the destination country and ask which form they will issue, **before** the goods ship. Changing or reissuing a certificate after departure is slow and sometimes not possible, which can leave a container sitting at the destination accruing demurrage.

## Verify before you file

Form-to-agreement mappings and issuing procedures change as trade agreements are renegotiated, and several widely circulated guides are out of date. Before filing, confirm the current requirements against the Ministry of Trade's own portal:

- [e-SKA portal, Ministry of Trade](https://e-ska.kemendag.go.id/home.php) — registration, application workflow and the official manual
- [Directorate General of Foreign Trade](https://ditjendaglu.kemendag.go.id/) — announcements and issuing-agency coordination

Your customs broker at the destination is the other check worth making, because the requirement that bites is usually the importing country's, not Indonesia's.
